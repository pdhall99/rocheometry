# Published tables

Each file is one published table, as printed, in [ECSV](https://github.com/astropy/astropy-APEs/blob/main/APE6.rst): a space-separated table under a commented YAML header, so the rows read like the printed page, and `astropy.table.Table.read` opens it.
The `literature_tables` study compares every value with Rocheometry: `hatch run accuracy:study literature_tables`.

| Source | Tables | Compared |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kuiper & Johnson (1956) | 1, 2 | L1 and L2, named lobe radii |
| Kopal (1959) | 3-1, 3-2 | L1 in Kopal's potential, the section's extent |
| Plavec & Kratochvil (1964) | 1–4 | L1, L2, L3, the L1 cone angle, axis radii, the section's extent |
| Mochnacki (1984) | 1–15 | L1, L2, the potentials of his fill-out grid, the volume radius, area and mean gravity of detached surfaces, and the total volume, area and mean gravity of contact envelopes |
| Guillochon & Prochaska (2026) | the 73-point reference solution | the lobe volume radius and L1 over qi = 1e-6 to 1e6 |
| Leahy & Leahy (2015) | Table 1, `RocheTable.txt`, `RocheLobe.f90` output | L1, L2, L3, named radii, lobe volume, radii in 120 directions and at fill-outs 0.1–0.95 |

## A table file

- The rows hold the values exactly as printed, as strings, separated by spaces (runs of spaces are fine, so columns may be aligned).
  Restoring a typographic omission such as a leading `0.` is allowed; changing a digit is not.
- The header's `meta` gives a short `reference` (as results cite the table), the full `sources`, each with its ADS `bibcode` where it has one and a `url`, says in `retrieval` how the values were read and checked, and gives the `conventions`: the mass parameter, frame and potential, so a row becomes a Rocheometry geometry.
- Each compared column's `meta` names a `rocheometry_quantity`.
  Columns Rocheometry can't compute carry a `status` instead and are skipped, as are rows listed under `row_status` (a printed q = 0 limit, say).
- Annotations are keyed by row (the mass ratio as printed) and column: `notes` explain disagreements (below), and `errata` keep the value originally printed where an erratum's correction is stored.
- Mochnacki divides a contact envelope by the plane through L1, Rocheometry along the gravity watershed, so only totals over both components compare.
  A primary's contact column lists its `partners`, the tables it is combined with at the same row and column, and the printed total's half-unit is the propagated rounding of every value in it.
- Leahy & Leahy publish their code, so two tables are generated by running `RocheLobe.f90` unmodified; their `retrieval` records its sha256 and the compiler.

## Agreement

Each value's error is `d = (Rocheometry − printed) / (half a unit in the last printed digit)`, and its row records a verdict:

| Verdict | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| agrees | \|d\| ≤ 1 |
| within allowance | \|d\| ≤ the table's `allowance_half_units`, given with its reason (a 1955 desk calculation, say). A column may have its own `allowance_half_units` or `allowance_relative` |
| explained | the value has a note with `class`, `reason`, `evidence` and `max_half_units`, and \|d\| is within it |
| unexplained | anything else: the row fails |

A note records evidence, such as a misprint confirmed by an independent high-precision solve, never just a disagreement; its `max_half_units` keeps guarding against regressions.
If an independent solve agrees with the source, Rocheometry is wrong: fix it instead.
A new table whose disagreements aren't explained yet can be marked `status: triage` in its `meta`, which turns its rows into notes.
